Privacy Policy

mailctl

Last updated: September 13, 2026

What mailctl is

mailctl is a personal command-line email client. It runs entirely on the machine of the person who installs it and reads and sends mail on behalf of that same person's own accounts. There is no hosted service, no shared backend, and no server operated by the developer that receives, stores, or processes your mail.

Google data mailctl accesses

When you connect a Gmail account, mailctl requests these OAuth scopes:

  • gmail.readonly — to list and display your messages in the terminal.
  • gmail.send — to send messages and replies that you compose.
  • gmail.modify — to move a message to the trash when you run the trash command.

mailctl requests no scope beyond the ones above, and uses each only for the command you explicitly run.

Where that data goes

  • Message content is fetched from the Google API and written to your terminal. It is not uploaded anywhere, and is not retained after the command finishes — except for attachments you deliberately choose to save to disk.
  • OAuth tokens are stored on your own filesystem, under ~/.config/mailctl/tokens, with owner-only file permissions.
  • If you enable the optional triage sync, only the identifiers of threads you marked as dismissed are synchronised, and only to infrastructure that you yourself operate and configure. No message content, subject, sender, or body is included.

What mailctl never does

  • It does not sell, rent, or share your data with anyone.
  • It does not transfer your data to any third party, advertiser, or data broker.
  • It does not use your data for advertising or for training any AI or machine-learning model.
  • It collects no telemetry, analytics, or usage statistics.
  • No human other than you ever reads your mail through mailctl.

Limited Use disclosure. mailctl's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Retention and deletion

Because all data stays on your own machine, you control its removal completely. Deleting the ~/.config/mailctl directory removes every stored token and all local state. To revoke mailctl's access to your Google account at any time, visit Google Account → Third-party access and remove mailctl. Revoking access takes effect immediately and does not require contacting the developer.

Security

Tokens are written with owner-only permissions and never leave your machine. All connections to Google and to mail servers use TLS. Where supported, mailctl can read credentials from an external password manager instead of storing them on disk at all.
